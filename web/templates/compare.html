    What can a comparison tell me?

    Today, this page compares saved notes and recording details, then lets you listen manually. It does not measure pitch, identify calls, or infer what an animal meant. An observation is not a translation; a sound after a person’s voice is not evidence of a reply.

    To see why these boundaries matter, read our review of a disputed bonobo call-combination claim. The original paper and a later published critique disagree about what the data support.

    Origin, animal context, clock and notes are saved declarations. File checks confirm internal consistency, not identity, consent or scientific validity. Device clocks are not synchronized. Highlights can overlap and omit quieter sounds; their summed audio length is not coverage.

    A future research path could test separate steps: acoustic properties; biological categories; scene timing and order; then matching against reference clips. Each result should stay linked to its recording and evidence, and none alone establishes understanding.

    Use original session ZIPs, up to 27 MB each. Repacked archives and Sound desk notebooks are different formats. Open a notebook in Review. Keep your original files: closing or leaving this page forgets the open copies.
