LARGE DATASET · METHODS PATH

Great Tits at Wytham

Best large-data path for learning song-structure methods. The labels describe songs and notes; they do not explain what a call means.

Research details

The public dataset describes 109,963 songs, more than 1.16 million annotated acoustic units and over 400 birds in one U.K. population. A 2025 study already used deep metric learning across the corpus to analyze repertoire similarity and song culture. Any new work must be a scoped reproduction or a clearly distinct question.

Nestbox and perimeter metadata still need a location-sensitivity review; no data files have been acquired.

CONTROLLED EXCHANGE · RESEARCH LEAD

Zebra-finches in ZF-AIM

The closest reviewed lead for studying turn-taking and measurable responses in controlled vocal exchanges—not a translated conversation.

Research details

A 2026 preprint reports extensive vocal-interaction experiments and changes in birds’ calling during interactive versus non-interactive playback. The separate data deposit has conflicting CC BY-SA and CC BY-NC declarations. Four raw example tracks do not establish that the complete analyzed corpus is available, and bird-level independence across splits remains unverified.

No data or model has been acquired or run.

COMMUNICATION-ADJACENT CONTEXT CANDIDATE

Egyptian fruit bats

A published context-prediction question, but its current connected groups do not support a balanced held-out evaluation.

Research details

A 2024 paper reports predicting video-coded interaction context from 35,074 vocalizations across 11 classes. These are published results, not a Talk2Nature reproduction, and do not show that bats understood a message.

The recordings used a specialized 250 kHz laboratory system, not ordinary phones. A metadata-only reconstruction matches the reported filtered count, not the exact rows or folds. Keeping each file and every positive known participant together leaves one group with 95.6% of examples. Specialist review and additional session/context metadata are needed; we have not trained a model.

PROVISIONAL PHONE-COHORT CANDIDATE

Budgerigars

A practical candidate for testing the phone workflow with existing carers; the app records observations and does not identify the animal.

Research details

Budgerigars have published research on learned contact calls and social interaction. Available public data are not a ready-made, event-aligned phone-audio-to-behavior training set.

A household study needs multiple independent homes, an avian-reviewed behavior codebook, consent and privacy safeguards, and a retention and withdrawal plan. No cohort has been recruited.

SEPARATE TECHNICAL BENCHMARK

Zebra finches: who called?

V33DA is a narrow caller-attribution task, not a communication test.

Research details

V33DA reports 33,625 physically verified call events from 10 zebra finches. Its labels support caller attribution among visible birds, not behavior, receiver response or call meaning. A read-only inventory of its pinned 100-event code sample lists 334 files totaling 62,503,976 bytes; the sample-specific split and several metadata joins remain unverified.

This is a candidate for a small methods exercise only if independent avian and ML reviewers approve the question and grouping. No sample data or model has been acquired or run.

Read the V33DA dataset and sample audit →

Keep sound detection separate from communication

Large bird and multi-species archives can help test whether software detects sound events or recognizes annotated species and call types. Those labels do not tell us what an animal meant. The new Northeastern forest-bird release is a promising field reference for detection, but its paper abstract and latest data version disagree on the number of species. Central European Birds is another detection candidate with a different label and rights profile. Neither is a behavior or receiver-response dataset. Dogs, cats, frogs and other animals remain welcome in the observation tools, without a claim that the app identifies every species.

A useful first model question

For a large-data methods exercise: can a model reproduce expert-marked song-note boundaries on Great Tit recordings from birds it has not seen? That tests an observable acoustic task, not meaning, and would need to be checked against the published methods before it is treated as new research. A separate future question is whether calls predict independently video-coded social context; the current bat candidate does not yet support a balanced grouped holdout.

For either task, freeze the labels and test set, keep related animals and sessions together, compare simple baselines, and have a qualified reviewer assess the design before training.

A prediction is a candidate association. A separate, ethically reviewed study measuring a receiver’s response would be needed to test a bounded communication claim. The Field Companion uses passive, user-started observation; it does not play calls to animals.
