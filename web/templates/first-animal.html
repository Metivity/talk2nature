CHOOSING OUR FIRST STUDY

Many animals.
Two careful starts.

Talk2Nature is built for observations across species. The first model question and the first phone-based study need not use the same animal.

Both choices remain provisional.

Existing evidence makes Egyptian fruit-bat interaction-context prediction our leading ML candidate. A separate, voluntary companion-animal cohort could start with budgerigars if household access and avian research support are available. Neither choice has passed the review, rights and protocol gates for new data collection or model training.

DATA-LED ML CANDIDATE

Egyptian fruit bats

A 2024 paper reports predicting video-coded interaction context from bat calls. It reports 35,074 vocalizations across 11 classes and 33.3% unweighted average recall for its best model. These are published results, not a Talk2Nature reproduction, and they do not show that bats understood a message.

The data came from a specialized 250 kHz laboratory recording system, not ordinary phones. A metadata-only reconstruction now matches the paper's reported 35,074 filtered examples, but does not confirm the exact rows or folds. Keeping each file and every positive known participant together leaves one group with 95.6% of examples, so a defensible split needs specialist review and additional session/context metadata. We have not trained a model.

PROVISIONAL PHONE-COHORT CANDIDATE

Budgerigars

Budgerigars have published research on learned contact calls and social interaction, making them a useful candidate for repeated, passive observations with existing carers. The available public data are not a ready-made, event-aligned phone-audio-to-behavior training set.

A household study would need multiple independent homes, a small behavior codebook reviewed by an avian specialist, consent and privacy safeguards, and a clear retention and withdrawal plan. No cohort has been recruited.

Keep sound detection separate from communication

Large bird and multi-species archives can help test whether software detects sound events or recognizes annotated species and call types. Those labels do not tell us what an animal meant. For example, Central European Birds is a candidate for sound-event detection; it is not a behavior or receiver-response dataset. Dogs, cats, frogs and other animals remain welcome in the observation tools, without a claim that the app identifies every species.

The first useful model question

Do naturally produced calls add information about an independently observed interaction context on animals and recording sessions the model has not seen? Test simple class-prior and recording-condition baselines first. Then compare audio-only predictions with context-only and combined models, using whole animals, pairs and sessions as the held-out units where the source data support them.

A prediction is a candidate association. A separate, ethically reviewed study measuring a receiver’s response would be needed to test a bounded communication claim. The Field Companion uses passive, user-started observation; it does not play calls to animals.
