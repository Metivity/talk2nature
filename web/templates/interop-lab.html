THE REPRODUCIBLE PART

Follow the sound.
Check what survives.

The app makes the package on your device. Audacity checks whether the common WAV and timed labels open together. Your result stays yours unless you choose to report it.

  1. 01

    Make the invented recording

    Open Listen and choose Try an example. You’ll see an eight-second waveform with tones at one to two seconds and four to five seconds.

    Open Listen →
  2. 02

    Mark both sounds

    Add two events at 1–2 s and 4–5 s. Set each to other sound; keep context unknown and source not observed. For the second note, try רגע 🌿, then a new line and second line.

  3. 03

    Carry it into Audacity

    Expand Continue in Audacity, download the package and unzip it. Open recording.wav, then use File → Import → Labels to import labels.txt.

    See the format and privacy notes ↗
  4. 04

    Compare, including the uncertainty

    Check the two label spans, the Unicode note and the visible \n escape. The full newline stays in annotations.json. Compare the source checksums in annotations.json and export-report.json. No playback is needed.

YOUR EXPECTED RESULT

A clean handoff
keeps the unknowns.

01

Two time spans

One from 1.000000 to 2.000000 seconds; one from 4.000000 to 5.000000 seconds.

02

One unchanged sound

The WAV remains eight seconds, mono, 16 kHz. Its checksum agrees with the source record.

03

Honest labels

Both are declared synthetic “other sound” events. Animal identity and context remain unknown.

04

Safe text, intact notes

Unicode appears in the label. A newline is escaped there so it cannot split a row, and stays a newline in the JSON record.

ONE OBSERVED CONFIGURATION

Audacity 4.0.1 · macOS · Apple silicon · checked October 6, 2026. The WAV opened and the two standard labels imported. Other versions, operating systems and audio workflows have not yet been checked.

This smoke test checks file handoff only. It does not validate detection accuracy, consent, species, behavior or meaning.
