# Proposed bird-observation pilot: does sound add information?

October 1, 2026. Discussion concept, not an approved protocol, recruitment or completed experiment.

**Question.** For one bird species and setting, do acoustic features improve prediction of an independently observable behavior compared with predeclared non-acoustic baselines on unfamiliar animals and sessions? Parrots are one candidate, conditional on leadership, access, welfare and rights. No Kiki code or data has been incorporated.

**First user.** A study organizer connecting recordings to observed behavior and reviewing ambiguous labels. First test whether this is a meaningful workflow problem and whether existing tools already solve it. Listen provides a local prototype for that conversation.

**Before collection.** A scientific lead selects species, setting, definitions, sampling, stopping rules, observer training, consent, storage and withdrawal. An independent reviewer examines the protocol. Power and uncertainty depend on the sampling design; 20 participants or 50 clips is not a sample-size justification. No enrollment or automatic playback starts from this concept.

**Observation.** Label behavior independently of audio predictions using synchronized video or direct observation with known timing. Preserve unknowns and disagreements. Track pseudonymous individuals, sessions, source checksums, equipment and permitted environmental context. Assess human speech, sensitive locations and bystanders. Intake stays closed until protocol, consent and hosted storage review are complete.

**Analysis after admission.** Freeze labels and a primary metric before evaluation. Start with a prevalence baseline and simple acoustic features; compare non-acoustic and background controls. Keep connected animals, sessions and sources together, adding site/device/time controls and checking relevant pretrained-data overlap. Hold the test set aside. Report class-specific errors and uncertainty at the independent group level. Choose the final metric and inferential design with the scientific reviewer; data may support only a feasibility report.

**Interpretation.** Predicting feeding does not show a vocalization means hunger. Association would justify a narrower follow-up, not general translation. Receiver-response experiments require separate scientific and welfare review and are outside this passive pilot.

**Deliverables.** Reviewed protocol/codebook; rights/provenance register; annotation-feasibility report; reproducible evaluation; independently reviewed result or an account of why the question cannot be answered. Release only permitted artifacts. Data, code, weights and articles have separate licenses.

**Current evidence.** Public research notes, synthetic examples, local annotation/metadata tools and a private owner-only metadata prototype. No biological evaluation, scientific partnership, trained model or funded grant is claimed.
