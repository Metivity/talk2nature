# Open-source practice and connection map

Checked October 1, 2026 for Talk2Nature. This is a public-source assessment, not an affiliation, acceptance, grant award or record of outreach. No prospective partner has been messaged for this task.

## Recommendation

Build a reusable observation and annotation workflow that one research group can validate. Keep the evidence library connected to methods and limitations. A demonstrable contribution to an existing scientific workflow is a stronger introduction than claiming a universal animal translator. Begin with practitioners and a scientific reviewer; approach UN networks with that bounded contribution and measured benefit.

Open-source software and open science overlap but are not identical. A code license permits reuse; community practice determines whether someone can actually contribute; a protocol, data provenance and independent evaluation determine whether scientific conclusions hold up.

| Sustainable stewardship | Explain review ownership and avoid unsupported response-time promises | Transparent funding terms and maintenance capacity; no purchases or incorporation this turn |
| Credible public benefit | Document proposed biodiversity/education benefits separately from results | Measured review time, label quality, task completion and reuse by an external team |

Sources reviewed: [GitHub maintainer guidance](https://opensource.guide/best-practices/) and [community guidance](https://opensource.guide/building-community/) for contribution/review processes; [UNESCO implementation framework](https://www.unesco.org/en/open-science/implementation) for inclusive open science; [DPG Standard](https://www.digitalpublicgoods.net/standard) for recognition requirements. These informed project choices; they do not certify this implementation.

Use the DPG checklist before nomination: SDG relevance, licenses, ownership, platform independence, documentation, non-personal data extraction, applicable privacy/legal requirements, standards and harm safeguards. Current documentation is stronger after this audit, but public-benefit evidence, operational safeguards and applicant identity are incomplete. Do not call Talk2Nature a recognized digital public good or assume DPGA recognition brings funding.

[FAIR](https://www.gofair.foundation/fair-principles) informs findable, documented and interoperable metadata; it does not require unrestricted disclosure. [GIDA's CARE principles](https://www.gida-global.org/careprinciples) add community authority and benefit where Indigenous data or knowledge is involved. Future research must be designed with the relevant communities, not merely extract recordings from their environments. Neither principle set substitutes for an actual consent/rights assessment.

## Before the first inquiry

Prepare a one-page brief: the passive-observation question, a working synthetic demo, what currently works, what is untested, component licenses, the exact review request, and what we will contribute back. Ask for a small piece of scientific or workflow feedback, not an endorsement. Record replies and permission before displaying any organization or person as a partner. Personalized messages remain a separate explicitly authorized action.

The first success measure is one useful review or external workflow test. UN visibility comes after a credible contribution; an institutional logo is not a substitute for it.
