# Benchmark candidates: context, call-category perception, identity and sound detection

**Status: review proposal only. No new data acquisition, model execution, or AutoResearch run is approved.** This card compares five distinct research exercises; they do not measure the same task.

## Decision

**No first communication-adjacent ML benchmark is selected or training-ready.** The zebra-finch call-category study is now the highest-value *review lead*: it links a published acoustic classification problem to measured discrimination behavior by the birds. A useful, still-unproven question is whether a model's errors on vocalizers held out as groups align with the call-category confusions measured in the operant task, beyond acoustic similarity. This must be checked against the 2026 BirdAVES classifier paper, which makes plain call-label prediction prior art, and against unresolved 45-versus-50 bird counts, data-version identifiers and trial-to-audio joins. It is not a selected benchmark, and agreement with trained discrimination would not establish spontaneous communication or translation.

Egyptian fruit-bat interaction-context prediction remains the closest current lead for naturally observed social-context labels. The 2024 paper reports subject-independent development/test folds, but Talk2Nature has not verified their membership or the audio-to-annotation/session joins. More importantly, the filtered annotation subset currently forms nine components when grouping source files plus all known participants (largest component 95.56%) and ten when grouping source files plus positive emitters (largest component 95.31%). Neither grouping supports a balanced strict whole-group holdout at this annotation granularity. The bat task is therefore a research-fit question, not the selected first benchmark. Independent bat-behavior and ML/statistical reviewers must approve a different defensible estimand and grouping plan before further acquisition or model execution. Context prediction would still not test receiver comprehension or call meaning.

OpenWhistle is a strong new candidate for a **within-pod acoustic-structure reproduction**, but it has whistle-type labels rather than independent behavior/context labels. Its session split keeps sessions apart while the same five known dolphins occur across the task; several signature-whistle classes are tied to named individuals. It therefore cannot establish performance on unseen dolphins or the meaning of a whistle. Keep it as a separate candidate until its data and model terms and a cetacean/ML review are resolved.

Keep the tracks distinct. CEB is a useful bird sound-event detection and research-software exercise, subject to its group and rights checks; it is not the best dataset for a communication question. The monk-parakeet work is a caller-identity/convergence reproduction, not a meaning test. For the public phone-based observation experience, the separate provisional plan remains one parrot species (budgerigars if access and an avian researcher are available); none of these laboratory or archive datasets makes that cohort decision for us.

V33DA is a separate candidate for caller attribution, with an explicit sample, leakage and review gate in the [V33DA run card](V33DA_SAMPLE_RUN_CARD.md). It does not replace the bat context-prediction question or make a communication-meaning claim.

**Related high-scale resource, not a context candidate:** iNatSounds reports about 230,000 recordings across 5,569 species and is useful as a species-classification/pretraining reference. Its labels are weak and file-level, with no independently observed behavior or receiver response. The pinned dataset README limits use to non-commercial research/education and prohibits redistribution of recordings; source-media licenses vary. It is therefore not admitted to the shared commercial-capable Talk2Nature benchmark path. See [the source-linked assessment](https://metivity.github.io/talk2nature/research/inat-sounds-2024/); no iNatSounds audio or weights were acquired or run.

## Candidate A — CEB sound-event detection

**Question.** On Central European bird soundscapes, can a frozen detection pipeline locate labeled vocal events and identify their annotated species better than a simple non-neural baseline when the evaluation recordings are held out by acquisition group?

**Exact release and rights.** Central European Birds, Zenodo v1.1, DOI [10.5281/zenodo.21821133](https://zenodo.org/records/21821133). The complete record is 49.7 GB. The `train_soundscape` archive is 46.5 GB and has 18,469 files / 62,298 annotations; its labels are point-centered with a fixed ±2.5-second window and many lack a vocalization-type label. The `test_soundscape` archive is 489.8 MB and has 147 recordings / 15,064 strongly labeled events. Soundscape audio and dataset annotations/metadata are declared CC BY 4.0. Exclude `train_xenocanto` from a first pass: each focal recording keeps its own Xeno-Canto terms, and some license entries are blank. Attribute the dataset and retain its provenance if any permitted files are later used.

**October 23 metadata gate.** The official v1.1 API lists `train_soundscape.csv` at 8,559,233 bytes (provider MD5 `55b5e3f99566c5bb3ba4fb5b833424da`) and `test_soundscape.csv` at 2,401,049 bytes (provider MD5 `f572155ae41714e17b218e95d97b1ce9`). These provider checksums are not locally verified; neither table's rows were inspected. The training table exceeds Talk2Nature's 5 MiB per-file collection limit, so do not bypass the cap by fetching or chunking the file directly. The smaller CSV contains public test labels and was left uninspected; it is not a concealed locked holdout. Keep this candidate metadata-only. Revisit only with an explicitly reviewed, bounded rights/privacy plan that fits an approved intake limit; CEB remains separate from the first bat-context question.

**What the labels establish.** They support annotated sound-event presence, species and vocalization categories. They do not provide independently observed social behavior, receiver response, or a ground truth for what a call communicates. The source record identifies 40 zero-area annotations and a truncated focal recording; resolve the zero-area rows for any localization analysis and exclude the focal subset from this initial soundscape-only plan.

**Grouping and evaluation proposal.** Use soundscape recordings only. Before selecting rows, inspect the licensed metadata table and establish what its anonymized `project` identifiers represent. The minimum group is the full source recording; keep all clips from one source project/acquisition together across partitions. A project label is only a proxy until a reviewer confirms the recording/site/device structure and checks overlap between the publisher's train and test subsets. The public `test_soundscape` labels are downloadable, so this set is not a private or durable secret holdout. It could be used for a one-time evaluation only if a named independent custodian verifies that the evaluation team has not inspected the labels, preserves the exact release/fingerprint and opens them once after the analysis is frozen. Otherwise treat it as development data and obtain an independent held-out set. Stop if the grouping keys cannot support the intended generalization claim.

**Baseline and measures.** Start with a prevalence/no-skill baseline and a simple log-mel feature classifier. A pretrained model is optional only after the exact binary, training-data overlap and model license are reviewed; do not assume the model-card license clears its inputs. Candidate primary measures are class-macro average precision for event detection and class-macro event F1 with thresholds chosen on validation only. For localization, report time-frequency intersection-over-union and event recall at reviewer-approved matching rules. Estimate uncertainty by resampling independent project/recording groups, not individual calls. Report per-species coverage and false positives per recorded minute; do not collapse these into a translation score.

**Proposed resource ceiling.** If approved, CPU only, no cloud or GPU, at most 2 GB of selected soundscape media, 4 CPU-hours, 8 GB RAM and 5 GB scratch space. The proposed acquisition limit is a planning cap, not proof that a statistically useful sample fits. If the group/label audit shows that it does not, stop and revise the design before acquiring audio.

## Candidate B — monk-parakeet acoustic identity reproduction

**Question.** Can we reproduce the published comparison of acoustic similarity at the individual and site scales from the exact deposited calls and analysis version?

**Exact release and rights.** The associated paper is Smith-Vidaurre et al. (2023), [PLOS Computational Biology](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011231). The linked dataset is Figshare v2, DOI [10.6084/m9.figshare.22582099.v2](https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/Smith-Vidaurre_et_al_2023_IdentityInformationEncoding/22582099), whose item metadata declares CC BY 4.0 and lists 22 files totaling 187,820,138 bytes. The article's CC BY notice, data item's license, analysis repository's GitHub MIT notice and linked Zenodo archive's `other-open` license field are separate. Audio-to-selection provenance and per-file rights have not been audited, and the archive has not been acquired. Keep this candidate metadata-only until those exact assets and scopes are reconciled.

**What the paper reports.** Its random forest was trained on 156 contact calls from eight known individuals. The reported 97.44% accuracy is training accuracy. The other 73 calls from nine individuals were used to derive similarity/proximity patterns and clustering, not to report held-out caller-classification accuracy. The paper combines acoustic-similarity methods and compares individual- and site-scale convergence; this is not an unseen-caller translation result.

**Reproduction and split proposal.** First reproduce the paper's stated similarity/convergence analysis, retaining its declared individual/site units and resampling procedure. The exact feature files, audio links, identities, exclusions and code/data version correspondence must be verified before fixing any partition. Do not invent a random call-level split. A new same/different-individual classifier would be a separate question: all calls from an individual must stay on one side of the split, with adequate repeated calls per held-out individual; the current reviewed evidence does not establish that this condition is met.

**Baseline and measures.** For a paper reproduction, match the published SPCC and random-forest-proximity analyses, then compare within- versus between-individual/site similarity using the paper's Earth Mover's Distance and its reported bootstrap approach. Include a simple chance/reference analysis, preserve individual/site as the resampling units and report every deviation. Do not reinterpret the original training accuracy as a held-out score. A new predictive task needs a separately specified baseline and metric after the data audit and reviewer decision.

**Proposed resource ceiling.** If rights and code scope are cleared, CPU only, no cloud or GPU, at most 2 CPU-hours, 8 GB RAM and 5 GB scratch space. This cap is provisional and cannot override a data/rights or scientific stop condition.

## Candidate C — Egyptian-fruit-bat interaction-context prediction

**Question.** Can audio predict video-coded interaction context for held-out bats, and does it add information beyond class frequency and recording-condition baselines? This would reproduce or challenge a published context-prediction result. It would not test whether a bat understood a signal or what a call “means.”

**Exact source and current inspection.** Prat et al.'s 2017 dataset descriptor reports 293,238 short WAV files recorded at 250 kHz and 91,080 annotations derived from video. The labels include emitter, addressee, context and actions before and after the vocal interaction. Talk2Nature has an aggregate-only audit of the complete annotation-table item: counts match the paper's table, but the table contains 87,986 distinct file IDs for 91,080 rows, so rows are not independent recordings. The associated `Metadata.pdf` was reviewed. A metadata-only reconstruction of the paper's stated filter (known context except landing, positive emitter, call duration at most three seconds) yields 35,074 rows across 34,603 source FileIDs, matching the published count. This is count-level agreement, not a row-by-row match to the paper's retained manifest. When each source file and every positive known participant are kept in one group, the filtered rows form nine connected groups; the largest contains 33,516 rows (95.56%), 33,048 files and 35 known participants. This is a structural warning against a balanced strict whole-file/participant holdout, not a verification or disproof of the paper's folds. The 3.05 GB audio archive and the separate 31.6 MB `FileInfo.csv` are not acquired; session, date, channel, treatment and chamber joins remain unverified.

**Filtered-subset grouping.** The existing aggregate report also groups by source FileID plus positive emitter only: ten connected components, with the largest containing 33,430 annotations (95.31%), 32,963 files and 29 emitters. Adding positive known addressees yields nine components, with the largest at 95.56%. Even the narrower caller-only grouping is too concentrated for a balanced whole-group holdout at this granularity. This does not reconstruct or disprove the paper folds; missing role, session, date, channel, treatment and chamber links remain unresolved.

**Published result and split.** Triantafyllopoulos et al. (EUSIPCO 2024) retain 35,074 vocalizations across 11 context classes. They report three subject-independent development/test folds; within each development fold, their 70/30 training/validation split is stratified by label but not identity. Pooled unweighted average recall is 22.4% for an SVM, 27.7% for ResNet-50 and 33.3% for EfficientNet-B0, versus about 9% chance for 11 classes. The reported intervals bootstrap individual examples, not animal or session clusters. These are published results, not a Talk2Nature reproduction. The exact partitions and any matching code/model release have not been verified.

**Rights and boundaries.** The Figshare API declares CC0 for the individual annotation item and for the separate audio archive item; this records repository declarations, not independent legal advice or a review of archive contents. Keep the paper, annotations, metadata PDF, audio archive, code and weights as separate assets. The arXiv v1 paper manuscript has its own CC BY-NC-ND terms; do not republish it. No bat audio or model weights have been acquired, and the existing annotation receipt explicitly keeps `training_admitted` false. The source dataset used specialized ultrasonic microphones at 250 kHz, so it is not a drop-in dataset for ordinary phone microphones.

**Main validity risks.** The full annotation audit finds marked class imbalance; the paper reports zero grooming recall and discusses possible annotation ambiguity, pup development and chamber acoustics. Some contexts are associated with different chamber setups. A subject-independent split is a useful safeguard, but its adequacy cannot be verified without the exact fold membership and joins. Because an interaction can contain both an emitter and addressee, any new split must check both roles, animal pairs, sessions, dates, channels, treatments and chamber conditions. A metadata-only model and shuffled-label controls are needed to test nuisance shortcuts.

**Evaluation proposal.** First verify provenance, rights, file IDs and the paper's exact filtered population without opening test audio or labels beyond the documented release. Ask an independent bat-bioacoustics/behavior reviewer and an ML evaluation reviewer to assess the estimand and grouping. If they approve a bounded reproduction, compare class-prior and recording-condition baselines with a simple acoustic baseline and the published model family; report per-class recall, macro UAR, clustered uncertainty and all exclusions. Keep the published folds as a historical reproduction target, not a new locked test. A new claim needs an animal/pair/session-held-out set that remains unopened until the analysis is frozen. Define a compute and storage cap only after the selected, rights-cleared audio subset and splits are known; do not fetch the full archive by default.

## Candidate D — OpenWhistle dolphin whistle-structure reproduction

**Question.** Can we reproduce expert-labeled whistle-type classification and whistle-presence detection on the published session-disjoint evaluation, and separately test temporal generalization if a reviewer confirms the available split metadata can support it? These are acoustic-structure tasks. They do not test social context, receiver response or message meaning.

**Exact source snapshots.** Mustun et al., [arXiv v1, 2609.34839](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34839v1), reports approximately 180,000 whistles and 114 hours collected over five years from five known dolphins at one semi-natural site, with 8,354 expert-labeled whistles across ten categories. The paper reports acceptance as a NeurIPS 2026 Datasets & Evaluations Spotlight. On October 23, the public Hugging Face metadata APIs returned these exact revisions: [OpenWhistle-Pretraining](https://huggingface.co/datasets/dolphinteam/OpenWhistle-Pretraining/tree/eea37423c7841b0f99a7c0380f945999c0a2c489) `eea37423c7841b0f99a7c0380f945999c0a2c489`; [OpenWhistle-Classification-Finetuning](https://huggingface.co/datasets/dolphinteam/OpenWhistle-Classification-Finetuning/tree/2b7806e3c5913c459c40b824ab7f944d494b3993) `2b7806e3c5913c459c40b824ab7f944d494b3993`; and [OpenWhistle-Wav2Vec2.0](https://huggingface.co/dolphinteam/OpenWhistle-Wav2Vec2.0/tree/985ac11bc6939af5e41383143aad5a0ae6993f68) `985ac11bc6939af5e41383143aad5a0ae6993f68`. The pretraining card describes 31,780 unlabeled 96 kHz segments totaling 114.284 hours; its repository reports 79.8 GB. The classification card exposes 8,354 labeled rows in the ten-class `all` config and a 3,000-row balanced six-class config. These are upstream card values, not a Talk2Nature inspection or reproduction.

**Rights and artifact boundaries.** The paper states CC BY 4.0 for OpenWhistle. The pinned Hugging Face dataset-card metadata returned no `license` value for either dataset repository, and the pinned model-card metadata also returned no `license` value; the model card text says its license has not been specified. Treat data, model and any code as separate, unresolved components until the maintainers clarify applicable terms. The datasets are public and ungated, which does not itself establish reuse permission. No audio, labels, code or model weights were acquired or run for this review.

**Evaluation boundary.** The paper and classification card describe 70/15/15 train/validation/test partitions with no recording-session overlap. The card reports 5,848/1,253/1,253 examples across the full ten-class set; an alternate balanced configuration contains six classes. A session-disjoint test still contains the same pod and named individuals across partitions, so it does not establish unseen-animal generalization. Seven signature-whistle categories identify particular dolphins, while three categories are non-signature types. Labels cover only part of the five-year span and are imbalanced; the published task is detection/classification, not context prediction or comprehension.

**Reproduction proposal and stop gate.** If component rights are clarified and independent cetacean-bioacoustics and ML-evaluation reviewers agree the task is useful, reproduce the frozen published split first using the paper's own metrics, then compare a simple spectral/MFCC baseline with the exact licensed released model. Preserve the ten-class and six-class tasks as separate evaluations. A temporal-shift analysis must be specified in advance and grouped by date/session; do not relabel a session split as a year-held-out test. Use the official test once after freezing the analysis, and report the result as within-site whistle-type discrimination. Do not acquire the 79.8 GB pretraining corpus, run the model, or create playback until permissions and a bounded research need are established.

**Current decision.** Do not rank the bat candidate first under the current grouping evidence. It remains the closest context-labeled lead, but its current connected groups are too concentrated for a balanced strict holdout, and no alternative grouping has been approved. The next gate is independent review of the bat estimand and the minimum artifacts needed to verify joins and groups. If reviewers cannot support a defensible evaluation, record a reasoned deferral and select a different task only after its own review. OpenWhistle is a separate acoustic-structure candidate whose data rights and evaluation conditions remain unresolved; these tasks must not be ranked by raw dataset size or presented as interchangeable evidence.

## Candidate E — zebra-finch model/receiver confusion alignment

**Question.** On the exact, version-matched zebra-finch call set, do a simple acoustic baseline and a frozen audio representation make held-out-vocalizer call-category errors that align with the birds' published discrimination errors beyond acoustic similarity?

**Why this is a review lead.** Elie et al. (2025) report 11 researcher-defined call categories, more than 8,000 vocal renditions from 45 vocalizers, and a trained discrimination task with 12 adult finches. The article reports that the birds' confusions track behaviorally related categories beyond the acoustic-similarity comparison. A later BirdAVES paper already studies classifier prediction of zebra-finch call categories, so plain call-type classification is prior art. The proposed contribution, if any, is the carefully matched comparison between model and animal error structure; the 2026 article and its split must be reconciled before claiming novelty.

**Version, rights and join gate.** The Science paper and UC Berkeley copy cite different Zenodo identifiers; the reviewed official API resolves them to an initial-submission archive and a July 2025 resubmission. The Figshare audio record lists 3,433 files and declares CC BY 4.0 at record level. Those records do not establish which exact release generated the final paper, how its audio files join to trial-level bird responses, or the source scope of every component. Reconcile the exact final-paper archive, file manifest, vocalizer IDs, trial/audio crosswalk, code and model terms before any acquisition or use. Keep article, data, code and weights as separate assets.

**Evaluation proposal and boundary.** If independent avian and ML/statistical reviewers approve the question, first reproduce the published call-label baseline and its exact reported split. Then determine whether a grouped held-out-vocalizer comparison is possible without leaking recordings or behavioral trials. Freeze call-level membership, baseline, model revision, metrics, uncertainty method and a development/test boundary before execution. A comparison is impossible if the published trials cannot be linked to the exact audio stimuli. The behavioral task is trained discrimination; predicted call categories or error alignment do not establish spontaneous exchanges, receiver understanding outside that task, or translation.

**Current decision.** Highest-value candidate for independent review, not selected or ready to run. The full article and record-level metadata have been reviewed, but supplementary methods, archive contents, exact joins and the BirdAVES split reconciliation remain incomplete. No audio, code or weights were acquired or run. AutoResearch remains **NOT READY** until the shared readiness gates below pass.

## Shared readiness gates

No run begins until all applicable gates have evidence:

1. A species-appropriate independent reviewer and a statistical/ML evaluation reviewer approve the exact question, sampling unit and claim boundary. Candidate A needs passive-acoustics expertise; Candidate B needs parrot vocal-behavior or convergence expertise; Candidate C needs bat-bioacoustics/behavior expertise; Candidate D needs cetacean bioacoustics or behavior expertise; Candidate E needs zebra-finch vocal-perception/behavior expertise. Screen authorship of the selected dataset and target result, and disclose relevant collaborations with their authors. Source-author replies are useful clarifications but do not satisfy the independent-review gate.
2. The exact versioned files, source checksums, item/file terms, attribution, privacy, retention and permitted-use scope are documented. Dataset, paper, code, dependencies and model weights remain separate assets.
3. The annotation guide, missingness, source provenance and any reliability evidence are reviewed. A model prediction is never used to create its own evaluation label.
4. The acquisition groups and near-duplicate/source-overlap risks are reviewed. Train/validation/test memberships are frozen and hashed before tuning; test access is controlled and logged. If no defensible independent holdout exists, the result is labeled a reproduction or exploratory analysis, not a benchmark.
5. A baseline, primary/secondary metrics, uncertainty procedure, seed schedule, compute cap, success/stop criteria and report template are registered before any model choice.
6. A local-only environment is reviewed for bounded data access, no credentials, no upload path and no animal playback. Preserve all failures and discarded trials if a later run is approved.

**Reviewer needed:** a bat-bioacoustics or animal-behavior specialist plus an independent ML/statistical-evaluation reviewer for Candidate C; a cetacean-bioacoustics/behavior specialist plus independent ML/statistical-evaluation review for Candidate D; an avian passive-acoustics reviewer for Candidate A; a parrot vocal-communication specialist for Candidate B; and a zebra-finch vocal-perception/behavior specialist for Candidate E. Candidate E also needs an independent ML/statistical-evaluation review of the animal-versus-model confusion comparison. For Candidates C and E, authors of the source studies can clarify their own materials but cannot satisfy the independent-review gate. Disclose relevant collaborations with source authors before characterizing a review as independent. No reviewer is currently assigned and no outreach has been sent.

**Current result:** the bat paper and aggregate annotation metadata have been reviewed, and the annotation table is held locally outside Git; no bat audio was acquired, no model was executed and no result was reproduced. Zebra-finch article and record-level metadata have also been reviewed, but archive contents, exact trial/audio joins and BirdAVES split/count reconciliation remain open. No independent reviewer has been contacted. The next highest-value gate is independent review of Candidate E's estimand and the minimum artifacts needed to establish a version-matched audio-to-trial crosswalk; Candidate C remains a separate context-prediction lead with a concentrated grouping structure. CEB's metadata/grouping audit remains a separate detector-engineering task. AutoResearch remains **NOT READY**. This card authorizes no new acquisition or model run.
